# Contract Review — NOT Legal Advice (Top-of-Output)

Used in `CONTRACT-REVIEW.md`. **Appears at the top of the output,
not buried at the bottom.** Reviewers must see it before reading
the red flags.

---

## Canonical text

> **THIS IS NOT LEGAL ADVICE.** This analysis was produced by an
> AI agent and is intended solely as a **NEGOTIATION-PREPARATION
> TOOL** to surface clauses worth discussing with qualified legal
> counsel in the relevant jurisdiction.
>
> The agent may misinterpret contract language, miss material
> clauses, or apply mainstream jurisdictional assumptions that do
> not match your situation. Risk-tier labels reflect generic
> commercial-contract experience, not your specific business,
> insurance, or regulatory context.
>
> **Do NOT sign or counter-sign any contract based solely on this
> review.** For deals of material value or unfamiliar structures
> (M&A, equity, regulated industries, cross-border), engage
> qualified legal counsel **before** negotiation, not after.
>
> The producing party accepts no liability for decisions made on
> the basis of this document.

## Why this language

CONTRACT-REVIEW is the highest unauthorised-practice-of-law (UPL)
risk in the library. Most jurisdictions have UPL statutes; some
(California, New York) interpret them strictly.

This disclaimer:
- Is the most prominent text in the output
- Uses "NEGOTIATION-PREPARATION TOOL" language to position the
  output as administrative, not legal
- Explicitly instructs "do not sign based on this"
- Names the consultant's lack of legal-professional capacity

## Mandatory placement

This text appears:
1. At the very top of the output (before §1 plain-language summary)
2. As its own §10 in the structured analysis
3. (Recommended) on the cover of any PDF export

## When to refuse the engagement

Do not run CONTRACT-REVIEW for:
- A contract the client intends to sign based on the output alone
- A contract in a jurisdiction where you cannot confirm UPL
  exposure
- A contract in a regulated industry (financial services,
  healthcare, defence) where specialised counsel is required
- Any contract for an M&A transaction
- Any equity / shareholder agreement

For those, recommend the client engage legal counsel first.
