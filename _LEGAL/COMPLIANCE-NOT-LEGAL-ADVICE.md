# Compliance Assessment — NOT Legal Advice

This text appears in the LIMITATIONS section of every regulatory
compliance prompt (GDPR, HIPAA, PCI-DSS, ISO 27001, DORA, AI Act,
mobile privacy labels, SBOM for CRA).

---

## Canonical text (substitute bracketed values per regulation)

> This assessment is NOT legal advice and does NOT constitute a
> [DPIA per GDPR Art. 35 / formal HIPAA risk analysis per §164.308 /
> SAQ or ROC attestation per PCI-DSS / certification audit per
> ISO 27001 / Notified Body conformity assessment per AI Act Art. 43
> / equivalent legal artefact]. It identifies technical and
> documented evidence of compliance posture; legal interpretation,
> organisational controls (training, sanctions, governance),
> contractual relationships with third parties, and operational
> execution require independent review by qualified counsel
> and / or accredited assessors in the relevant jurisdiction.
>
> Reliance on this report for [submission to a supervisory authority /
> attestation to customers / certification by an accredited body]
> requires that the recipient organisation's [DPO / CISO / Compliance
> Officer / qualified counsel] adopts the findings as their own after
> independent review.
>
> Regulatory standards evolve. This report reflects the regulatory
> state as of [DATE]; subsequent technical standards (RTS / ITS /
> implementing acts / national transpositions / enforcement guidance)
> may add obligations or clarify ambiguities not captured here.

## Why this language

- "NOT legal advice" must appear verbatim — courts read this strictly.
- Naming the specific equivalent legal artefact (DPIA, ROC, etc.)
  prevents ambiguity about what the report does and does not replace.
- The "adoption by client's qualified person" clause transfers
  professional responsibility correctly: the consultant produces an
  assessment, the client's qualified officer formally adopts it.
- Time-stamping the regulatory reference protects against future
  rule changes.

## Do not soften

Buyers will sometimes ask for the disclaimer to be removed or
softened "to keep the document clean for the board." Refuse. The
disclaimer is what protects you when the regulator or court reads
the document later.
