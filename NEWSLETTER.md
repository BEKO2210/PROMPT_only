# NEWSLETTER — Wöchentlicher/Monatlicher Content aus eigener Arbeit (verkaufbares Deliverable)

Turns your engagement work, learnings, and observations into a
recurring newsletter that builds inbound demand. Newsletters are the
highest-leverage owned channel: nobody can throttle your reach to
your subscribers. This produces both the editorial calendar and the
issues themselves.

**Realistic engagement price: €300 – 1 500 per issue, often recurring
(€1 200 – 6 000/month for weekly).**

---

## How to use

Paste the block below, then on the next line:

    NEWSLETTER_NAME: <name + one-line description>
    AUDIENCE: <who reads it — role, industry, awareness stage>
    CADENCE: <WEEKLY | BI_WEEKLY | MONTHLY>
    LENGTH: <SHORT (≤500 words) | MEDIUM (500–1200) | LONG (1200–2500)>
    VOICE: <conversational / authoritative / playful / dry>
    SOURCES: <raw material — engagement notes, observations, articles read, customer questions>
    GOAL: <what it drives — bookings, replies, awareness, link-clicks>

## The prompt (copy from here)

```
You are producing a newsletter issue from raw source material. Three
deliverables: §6 SUBJECT-LINE OPTIONS, §7 ISSUE DRAFT, §8 NEXT-ISSUE
TEASER.

Three rules:

(1) ONE big idea per issue. Two ideas dilute. Readers should be
    able to explain the issue in one sentence after reading.
(2) Conversational > corporate. Newsletters are the one channel
    that's allowed to be personal — leverage it. "Hi all" beats
    "Dear Subscribers".
(3) Reply rate is the real metric. CTR and open rate are noisy;
    a thoughtful reply is a strong signal. Write to provoke
    replies, not clicks.

1. THE ONE-IDEA SELECTION
   From SOURCES input, identify the strongest single thread:
     - A surprising insight from recent work
     - A common question multiple customers asked
     - A pattern emerging across engagements
     - A new framework / model worth sharing
     - A piece of news + your unique take
   Reject candidates that don't pass: "Would a subscriber forward
   this to one colleague?" If no, find a better idea.

2. ISSUE STRUCTURE  (the LENGTH dictates section choice)

   SHORT (≤500 words):
     - Hook (1–2 sentences)
     - One observation / insight
     - One actionable takeaway
     - One question for the reader (drives replies)
     - Sign-off

   MEDIUM (500–1 200 words):
     - Hook
     - Context (why this matters now)
     - The insight / story / framework
     - Concrete example or worked case
     - What this means for the reader
     - Question or CTA
     - Sign-off

   LONG (1 200–2 500 words):
     - Hook + TL;DR
     - Story (the engagement, the observation, the moment)
     - Insight (the pattern / lesson / framework)
     - Implications (for the audience specifically)
     - Concrete recommendations
     - Counterpoints / honest limits
     - Question or CTA
     - Sign-off + P.S.

3. SUBJECT LINES
   Newsletter subject lines work differently than cold email:
     - Curiosity / specificity, not benefit-stuffing
     - Lowercase often (feels personal)
     - 4–8 words ideal
     - First-name fields disabled (the "Hi {{Name}}" subject is dead
       in 2026)
     - Test: 3–5 variants per issue
   Bad: "5 Tips to Improve Your Marketing"
   Good: "i was wrong about funnels"

4. OPENING HOOK
   The first 1–2 sentences must earn the rest. Patterns that work:
     - In-medias-res story ("Last Tuesday I was on a call with X
       who said something that changed how I think about Y…")
     - Counterintuitive claim ("Everyone says do X. After 50
       engagements, I think the opposite.")
     - Direct question ("Have you ever wondered why Y always
       happens before X?")
     - Specific number with surprise ("We measured. The lift was
       73% — and the cause wasn't what we expected.")
   Bad opening patterns: "I hope you're well" / "It's been a busy
   week" / "I wanted to share…"

5. CTA DISCIPLINE
   - One CTA per issue (book a call / reply / share / read)
   - Reply-driven CTAs build relationship more than click-driven
   - "If this resonated, just reply 'yes' — I read every one" is
     the highest-leverage CTA most newsletters underuse
   - Calendar links if the GOAL is bookings — but only after value
     has been delivered in the issue, never before

6. SUBJECT-LINE OPTIONS  ← deliverable (a)
   5 distinct subject lines with predicted-driver classification
   (curiosity / specificity / personal / question / number).
   Recommended primary + 4 A/B variants.

7. ISSUE DRAFT  ← deliverable (b)
   --- ISSUE START ---
   **Subject:** <chosen primary>
   **Preheader:** <30–80 chars — appears next to subject in inbox>

   <Body per §2 structure>

   <Sign-off — first-name / your name / role>

   <P.S. — where it earns; CTA reinforcement or callback to the
   main idea or one-line teaser for next issue>

   <Footer block: unsubscribe link, physical address, archive link>
   --- ISSUE END ---

8. NEXT-ISSUE TEASER  ← deliverable (c)
   One sentence at end of this issue: "Next week: <hint at next
   topic>." Builds anticipation, reduces unsubscribes.

9. EDITORIAL CALENDAR  (if recurring — for next 4–8 issues)
   - Issue date
   - Working title
   - The §1 one-idea-candidate
   - Source material it draws from
   - Status (idea / drafting / scheduled / sent)
   This prevents the "what should I write about?" panic before each
   issue.

10. COMPLIANCE
    Every issue includes:
      - Visible unsubscribe link (CAN-SPAM, CASL, GDPR/PECR all
        require)
      - Physical sender address (CAN-SPAM; recommended elsewhere)
      - Honest preview text (no misleading subject lines)
      - Pre-existing subscriber relationship (no scraped lists —
        spam-trap territory)

HARD RULES
- One idea per issue. If two ideas surface from SOURCES, save one
  for next issue.
- Reply-CTA preferred over click-CTA when GOAL allows. Replies
  build relationships; clicks build vanity metrics.
- Subject lines that resemble cold-email patterns are banned —
  newsletters are an in-relationship channel.
- Sources used must be cited (links to articles, customer quotes
  must be approved, statistics must have origins).
- Respond in the language of the AUDIENCE input.

NEWSLETTER_NAME:
AUDIENCE:
CADENCE:
LENGTH:
VOICE:
SOURCES:
GOAL:
```

## What you get

Per issue: 5 subject-line options, the full draft, a teaser for next
issue, and a rolling editorial calendar. As a recurring service
(€1 200–6 000/month for weekly), this transforms a consultant's
engagement work into a compounding inbound asset — most successful
consultants attribute 30–60% of inbound leads to their newsletter.
