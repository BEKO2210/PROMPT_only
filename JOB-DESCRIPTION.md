# JOB-DESCRIPTION — JD die anzieht (statt HR-Bürokratie) (verkaufbares Deliverable)

Most job descriptions are written by HR for HR. The result: A-players
don't apply, B-players do, and the team complains about candidate
quality. This produces a JD that reads like the founder wrote it —
honest about the work, specific about who fits, transparent about
compensation.

**Realistic engagement price: €500 – 2 000 per role.** Often part of
talent-strategy retainer.

---

## How to use

Paste the block below, then on the next line:

    ROLE: <title, level, full-time/contract>
    TEAM: <which team, manager role, team size, team mission>
    PROBLEM: <what specific problem this hire solves>
    MUSTS: <skills / experience genuinely required>
    NICES: <skills that would be bonus>
    COMPENSATION: <range + equity + benefits — be transparent>
    LOCATION: <remote / hybrid / in-office; jurisdictions OK to hire from>
    COMPANY: <name, stage, what you do>

---

## The prompt (copy from here)

```
You are producing a job description that attracts top candidates.
The §8 JD is the deliverable. Three rules:

(1) WRITE LIKE THE FOUNDER WROTE IT. HR-templated JDs sort to
    "skip" by anyone with options. First-person, specific,
    honest = signals a team worth joining.
(2) HONEST ABOUT THE WORK. The actual day-to-day, the real
    challenges, what the role isn't. Bait-and-switch JDs produce
    quick exits.
(3) COMPENSATION IS TRANSPARENT. Posting a range raises
    application quality 30–50% (multiple studies). Hiding it
    selects for desperate candidates. Many jurisdictions now
    require it (NYC, California, Colorado, Washington, EU
    Pay Transparency Directive).

1. ROLE ARCHITECTURE
   - What specific outcome is the hire responsible for?
   - What decisions will they own?
   - What does success look like at 3 / 6 / 12 months?
   - Who do they work with internally + externally?
   - What's the seniority signal (IC vs lead; reports to whom)?

2. WHY THIS ROLE EXISTS  (the problem)
   The 2–3 sentence "why now":
     - What is currently broken or missing?
     - What's the cost of not hiring?
     - What's the opportunity if it's done well?
   This is the most-read paragraph. Make it real, not corporate.

3. WHO YOU ARE  (positive selection)
   The 4–8 attributes the hire needs:
     - Skills / experience (MUSTS verbatim — without inflating to
       sound prestigious)
     - Mindset / approach (the way of working that fits this team)
     - Stage fit (early-stage scrappy vs scale-up specialist)
   Avoid:
     - "10+ years experience" if role can be done by 5+ (gates
       out qualified people)
     - "Rockstar / ninja / wizard" (anti-signal in 2026)
     - Generic "team player / self-starter" (says nothing)

4. WHO YOU'RE PROBABLY NOT  (negative selection)
   Saves everyone time:
     - "If you want X, this isn't the role"
     - "If you need Y, we can't provide it"
   Examples:
     - "If you want a clearly-defined product roadmap to execute,
       this isn't it — we'll be inventing the roadmap together."
     - "If you need a senior architect to guide you, our team is
       small — you'll be the senior person on this surface."
   This filters costs both sides: candidates who self-deselect
   save you interview cycles.

5. DAY-TO-DAY
   What the work actually looks like:
     - Typical week's mix of activities
     - Tools / stack
     - Meeting load (be honest — heavy or light?)
     - Pace (sprint culture vs steady)
     - Where decisions get made (consensus / RACI / leader call)

6. COMPENSATION & BENEFITS  (transparent)
   - Salary range (transparent — top of range realistic for top
     candidates, not aspirational)
   - Equity (if applicable: % range, vesting structure, refresh
     policy)
   - Benefits (health, parental leave, time off, learning budget,
     remote stipend)
   - Pay-equity statement (we pay X for the same role regardless
     of location / negotiation)
   In EU: comply with Pay Transparency Directive (2023/970,
   member-state transposition mostly 2026).

7. INTERVIEW PROCESS  (preview)
   - How many stages, what each tests for
   - Total time investment for candidate (be honest — 8h test
     project + 4 panels is a lot)
   - Decision timeline from application to offer (be honest —
     6 weeks is 6 weeks, not "fast")
   - Who's on the panel

8. JOB DESCRIPTION  ← the deliverable
   --- JD START ---
   # <ROLE TITLE>
   *<Team> · <Location with options> · <Salary range> · <FT/Contract>*

   ## Why we're hiring
   <§2 — the real problem, in plain language>

   ## What you'll do
   <§1 outcomes + §5 day-to-day, combined into 4–6 bullets that
   feel concrete, not generic>

   ## What success looks like
   - In 3 months: <…>
   - In 6 months: <…>
   - In 12 months: <…>

   ## Who we're looking for
   <§3 attributes, 4–8 bullets>

   ## Probably not a fit if
   <§4 negative selection, 2–4 bullets>

   ## What we offer
   - <§6 compensation with range>
   - Equity: <…>
   - <Benefits>

   ## The process
   <§7 stages with honest time estimates>

   ## How to apply
   <Specific instructions — what to send, where to send, by when>

   ## About <COMPANY>
   <2–3 sentences. Honest. No "world-changing" if you ship
   accounting software.>

   ## A note on equal opportunity
   <Standard or jurisdiction-specific>
   --- JD END ---

HARD RULES
- Compensation range visible. Where law allows hiding it, still
  show it — it's competitive advantage in 2026.
- "10+ years required" is justified or removed. Inflated
  experience requirements gate out qualified candidates and
  produce a homogeneous candidate pool.
- §4 negative selection is mandatory — saves interview cycles for
  both sides.
- "Rockstar / ninja / wizard / guru" appears zero times. These are
  anti-signals.
- Respond in the language of the COMPANY input.

ROLE:
TEAM:
PROBLEM:
MUSTS:
NICES:
COMPENSATION:
LOCATION:
COMPANY:
```

## What the buyer gets

A JD that produces measurably better candidate pipeline: higher
quality, more self-selection (saves interview hours), faster time-
to-hire. Companies switching from template JDs to founder-voice JDs
typically see 2–4× improvement in qualified-applicant rate.
