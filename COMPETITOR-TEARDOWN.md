# COMPETITOR-TEARDOWN — Strukturierter Wettbewerber-Tear-Down (verkaufbares Deliverable)

Deep teardown of a competitor (or your own product against a competitor):
positioning, pricing, page structure, copy choices, growth signals
(hiring, ads, social), and a "what we'd steal" + "what we'd avoid"
playbook. Used by marketing teams quarterly to stay sharp; by founders
during fundraising to articulate moat.

**Realistic engagement price: €1 000 – 5 000 per teardown.** Often as
part of a competitive-intelligence retainer.

---

## How to use

Paste the block below, then on the next line:

    COMPETITOR: <legal name, primary URL, category>
    OUR_PRODUCT: <your product for comparison context — optional but recommended>
    SCOPE: <which axes to teardown: positioning / pricing / product / marketing / hiring / all>
    AUDIENCE_OVERLAP: <do you serve the same buyer? mostly / partially / different>
    CLIENT: <client name — optional>

---

## The prompt (copy from here)

```
You are producing a Competitor Teardown. The §10 REPORT is the
deliverable. Three rules:

(1) Public sources only. Pricing pages, marketing site, public
    LinkedIn, public job posts, public funding databases, ad
    libraries. Never speculate about private financials or
    internal practices.
(2) Cite every source URL. The credibility of competitive
    intelligence is the source trail.
(3) Steal-and-avoid format. The deliverable is not a feature
    matrix; it's a playbook of what to copy (because it works)
    and what to skip (because it's a trap).

1. COMPANY SNAPSHOT
   - Legal name, founded, HQ, key offices
   - Stage (bootstrapped / Series A–D / public)
   - Estimated employees (LinkedIn ±30%)
   - Funding history (Crunchbase-class public data)
   - Recent material news (last 12 months — pivots, layoffs, M&A,
     hires)
   - One-sentence current pitch (from their hero)
   Cite source URL for every claim.

2. POSITIONING
   - Category they place themselves in
   - Target ICP (inferred from hero copy, case studies, ad
     targeting if visible)
   - Stated differentiator (what their copy claims sets them apart)
   - Implicit differentiator (what their product / pricing /
     packaging actually suggests)
   - Mismatch flag if stated ≠ implicit

3. PRICING
   - Tier structure (number, names, prices)
   - Pricing model (per-user / usage / flat / hybrid)
   - Anchor strategy (what's their highest visible price?)
   - Free tier / trial / freemium policy
   - Discounts visible (annual, multi-year, non-profit, startup)
   - Pricing relative to category benchmarks (above / at / below)
   - "Talk to sales" threshold

4. PRODUCT  (what's visible publicly)
   - Core feature set (from product pages, changelog, docs)
   - What's notably MISSING (vs category norms)
   - Integration ecosystem (their integrations page)
   - API / developer presence (docs quality, SDK coverage)
   - Mobile / desktop / web availability
   - Recent product changes (changelog, blog, social)

5. MARKETING
   - Top channels visible (organic SEO content volume, paid ads
     spotted, social presence by platform)
   - Content cadence (blog posts/month, videos/month, newsletter)
   - Top-performing organic pages (use Ahrefs / Semrush if
     available — else use SEO heuristics on URL structure and
     site search results)
   - Ad spend signals (Meta Ad Library, Google Ads Transparency)
   - Conference / event presence
   - Partnership / co-marketing visible

6. CUSTOMER PROOF
   - Named customers (logos, case studies, testimonials)
   - Customer concentration risk (do all case studies come from
     one industry / region?)
   - Reviews on G2 / Trustpilot / Capterra / App Store:
     - Star rating + review count
     - Top complaints (the lever for your own positioning)
     - Top praise (the table-stakes you must match)

7. HIRING SIGNALS
   - Open roles count + functional split (engineering / sales /
     marketing / customer-success)
   - Roles indicating direction:
     - Many sales roles → going up-market / enterprise push
     - Many engineering roles in a specific area → product
       investment focus
     - SRE / DevOps roles → scaling / reliability pressure
     - Specific tech-stack roles → tech migration / new initiative
   - Location of new offices (geographic expansion)
   - Recent senior hires (LinkedIn) — what they did before signals
     strategic direction

8. STRENGTHS & WEAKNESSES  (the steal-and-avoid playbook)
   Strengths to STEAL (with rationale):
     - Page / copy / feature / motion that demonstrably works
     - Why it works
     - How OUR_PRODUCT could adopt without copying brand
   Weaknesses to AVOID or EXPLOIT:
     - Public failures / customer complaints / gap in offering
     - Strategic mistakes visible (over-pivoting, slow ship,
       brand erosion)
     - Where OUR_PRODUCT can directly position against

9. POSITIONING RECOMMENDATIONS  (if OUR_PRODUCT given)
   How OUR_PRODUCT should position relative to COMPETITOR:
     - Where to compete head-on (we're better at X)
     - Where to compete differently (we serve Y they ignore)
     - Where to NOT compete (their forte; not worth fighting)
     - 2–3 specific copy / page / messaging changes to make this
       week

10. REPORT  ← the deliverable
    --- REPORT START ---
    # Competitor Teardown — <COMPETITOR>
    **Date:** <…>   **Prepared for:** <CLIENT / OUR_PRODUCT>

    ## Executive Summary             (≤300 words: top 3 things to
                                      steal, top 3 things to avoid,
                                      one positioning shift to make
                                      this quarter)
    ## Company Snapshot              (§1)
    ## Positioning                   (§2)
    ## Pricing                       (§3)
    ## Product                       (§4)
    ## Marketing                     (§5)
    ## Customer Proof                (§6)
    ## Hiring Signals                (§7)
    ## Strengths & Weaknesses        (§8 — the steal-and-avoid
                                      playbook)
    ## Recommendations for OUR_PRODUCT (§9)
    ## Sources                       (every URL referenced, one list)
    ## Limitations                   (§11, verbatim)
    --- REPORT END ---

11. LIMITATIONS & DISCLAIMER  (mandatory, verbatim with substitutions)
    "This teardown was produced by an AI agent on <date> from
     publicly available sources only — no private intelligence,
     no insider information, no claims about non-public financials
     or internal strategy. Inferences from public signals (hiring,
     marketing spend, customer base) are advisory; the competitor's
     actual strategy may differ. Pricing reflects what is publicly
     listed; enterprise / negotiated deals are not visible.
     Customer-review samples are subject to platform-specific
     biases. This document is intended for internal strategic
     planning by the recipient and should not be redistributed
     without removing competitive-sensitive recommendations."

HARD RULES
- Every claim cites a public source URL. No second-hand rumours,
  no speculation about internal practices.
- Steal-and-avoid playbook is OPINIONATED — generic feature
  comparisons are useless; this is the value of the engagement.
- §9 recommendations to OUR_PRODUCT are SPECIFIC enough to act on
  this week (a page change, a copy line, a messaging shift) — not
  abstract "differentiate harder".
- §11 disclaimer is verbatim. Competitive intelligence circulated
  carelessly creates legal exposure.
- Respond in the language of the CLIENT input.

COMPETITOR:
OUR_PRODUCT:
SCOPE:
AUDIENCE_OVERLAP:
CLIENT:
```

## What the buyer gets

A 15–30 page teardown with steal-and-avoid playbook. The §9
recommendations are usually 2–3 messaging or page changes the team
ships within the week — that's where the engagement pays for itself.
