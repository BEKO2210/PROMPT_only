# TESTIMONIAL-REQUEST — Testimonial-Sammlung mit Approval (interner Hebel)

Generates the entire testimonial-collection process from ask to use:
when to ask, what questions to ask, draft-for-them template, written-
permission language, video-vs-written split, and the LinkedIn-recom-
mendation request. Testimonials are marketing oxygen — most
consultants under-collect by 5–10×.

**Leverage value: one strong testimonial generates inbound for 12+
months. Marginal cost: 15 minutes per engagement.**

---

## How to use

Paste the block below, then on the next line:

    ENGAGEMENT: <name + outcome>
    CLIENT_CONTACT: <name + role + organisation>
    RELATIONSHIP: <STRONG | NEUTRAL>
    USE_INTENT: <where you want to publish: website / proposal / LinkedIn / video / case study>
    SPECIFICS: <2–3 concrete outcomes you want them to mention>

---

## The prompt (copy from here)

```
You are producing a testimonial collection package. Four deliverables:
§4 ASK EMAIL, §5 DRAFT-FOR-THEM TEMPLATE, §6 PERMISSION LANGUAGE,
§7 LINKEDIN RECOMMENDATION REQUEST.

Three rules:

(1) ASK WHEN VALUE IS FRESH. 1–4 weeks after final deliverable
    lands. Too early = outcomes not visible yet; too late = momentum
    gone.
(2) MAKE IT EASY. Most clients say "I'd love to but I'm busy" —
    so DRAFT IT FOR THEM. Their job becomes "edit and approve",
    not "write from scratch".
(3) PERMISSION IS BINARY. Get explicit written approval on the
    exact text + the exact venues you'll use it. Verbal approval
    is a future dispute.

1. TIMING
   - 1–2 weeks after final deliverable acceptance is sweet spot
   - Avoid asking during the engagement (changes power dynamic)
   - Avoid asking before measurable outcomes are visible (vague
     testimonials are worse than no testimonials)

2. QUESTIONS TO ANCHOR THEIR RESPONSE  (for unstructured asks)
   Structured questions that produce good quotes:
     - "What was the situation before we started?"
     - "What specifically changed?"
     - "What's one concrete number, outcome, or moment that
       captures the value?"
     - "What would you tell a peer considering similar work?"
     - "Would you do it again? Why?"
   The third question is the gold-mining one — specific number /
   moment = quotable.

3. DRAFT-FOR-THEM STRATEGY
   For busy clients (most of them), offer to draft. Pattern:
     - Pull 2–3 sentences from your STATUS-REPORT / KICKOFF /
       SPECIFICS that capture the value
     - Write in the client's voice (their typical sentence
       length, formality, jargon)
     - Send the draft with explicit "edit anything, this is
       your voice" framing
   Typical conversion: 60–80% will return the draft with minor
   edits and approval — far higher than 10–20% who write from
   scratch.

4. ASK EMAIL  ← deliverable (a)
   --- EMAIL START ---
   **Subject:** Quick favour about <ENGAGEMENT>

   Hi <first name>,

   Now that <ENGAGEMENT> has wrapped, I'm hoping you'd be open
   to a short testimonial.

   It would help me massively — both for the website and for
   future <CLIENT-LIKE> companies wondering if this kind of work
   is worth it for them.

   Two ways to make this easy:

   **Option A — Quick reply:**
   What would you tell a peer who's considering similar work?
   Even 2–3 sentences would be enormously helpful.

   **Option B — I'll draft it:**
   I can draft something based on what we did and the outcomes
   we hit. You edit anything you want, approve the final, and
   we're done in 5 minutes. Reply "Draft" and I'll send a draft
   within 24h.

   No pressure either way — and if there's anything that would
   make it easier (a specific format, video, LinkedIn recommendation
   instead), let me know.

   Thanks for considering it.

   <signature>
   --- EMAIL END ---

5. DRAFT-FOR-THEM TEMPLATE  ← deliverable (b)
   When client picks Option B, the draft you send:

   --- DRAFT START ---
   **Subject:** Draft testimonial — edit anything

   Hi <first name>,

   Drafted three options. Pick any, edit anything, or write
   over the top. None of these are sacred.

   **Option 1 — Short, outcome-focused:**
   "<2 sentences citing one specific SPECIFICS outcome>."

   **Option 2 — Longer, narrative:**
   "<3-4 sentences: before, what we did, the outcome>."

   **Option 3 — Peer-focused:**
   "<Recommendation to a peer in their position, with the
   specific reason>."

   Reply with your edits + a yes/no on whether I can use:
   - On <DOMAIN.com>'s case-studies page (with your name, role,
     company logo): YES / NO
   - In sales proposals (with same attribution): YES / NO
   - On LinkedIn (I'll post and tag you): YES / NO

   Thanks!

   <signature>
   --- DRAFT END ---

6. PERMISSION LANGUAGE  ← deliverable (c)
   Once they approve, capture explicit permission. For high-stakes
   testimonials (named, with logo, public-facing), include:
   --- PERMISSION START ---
   Confirming: I, <name>, in my capacity as <role> at <company>,
   approve the use of the following text:

   "<approved testimonial text verbatim>"

   For use:
   ✓ On <domain>'s public website
   ✓ In <consultant company>'s sales proposals and pitch decks
   ✓ In LinkedIn posts authored by <consultant>, tagging me
   [☐ Other: specify]

   With my name and role as displayed above, and my company's
   name and logo (logo file attached / on file).

   This permission is granted for 24 months from <date>, after
   which renewal will be requested.

   <name>   <date>
   --- PERMISSION END ---

   For lower-stakes (LinkedIn recommendation, written reply
   testimonial), the email thread itself with explicit "yes you
   can use this" is sufficient — archive it.

7. LINKEDIN RECOMMENDATION REQUEST  ← deliverable (d)
   When and how to ask for a LinkedIn recommendation (separate
   asset from web testimonials — different audience, different
   social value):
   --- REQUEST START ---
   **Subject:** Would you write me a LinkedIn recommendation?

   Hi <first name>,

   Now that we've wrapped up <ENGAGEMENT>, would you consider
   writing me a short LinkedIn recommendation?

   To make it easy, here's a starting point you can use, edit,
   or ignore:

   "<consultant first name> led <ENGAGEMENT> for us at <company>.
   <Specific outcome>. <One adjective + reason — clear
   communicator, deeply technical, made the process feel
   collaborative>. I'd recommend <consultant>'s work to anyone
   facing <similar challenge>."

   To send, here's the link: https://linkedin.com/in/
   <your-handle>/edit/forms/new-profile/recommendations/

   Thank you — this is the kind of thing that compounds over
   years for me.

   <signature>
   --- REQUEST END ---

HARD RULES
- Drafts written in CLIENT'S voice (use STATUS-REPORT or KICKOFF
  emails as voice reference), not consultant's voice.
- Permission is captured in writing for every public use.
  Verbal permission expires when the client changes roles.
- Specific numbers / moments from SPECIFICS input appear in the
  drafts — generic "great work" testimonials damage credibility
  more than they help.
- LinkedIn recommendation is a separate ask, not bundled into the
  web-testimonial ask — different platforms, different audiences,
  separate opt-ins.
- Respond in the language of the CLIENT_CONTACT input.

ENGAGEMENT:
CLIENT_CONTACT:
RELATIONSHIP:
USE_INTENT:
SPECIFICS:
```
