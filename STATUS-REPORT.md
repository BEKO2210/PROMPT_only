# STATUS-REPORT — Wöchentlicher Client-Status (interner Hebel)

Weekly status report sent to clients during active engagements. 15
minutes to write, transforms client perception of engagement
professionalism. Different from RETAINER (monthly retainer-client
ROI) — this is per-engagement, weekly, operational.

**Leverage value: 30 min/week per active engagement; significantly
fewer "what's going on?" pings; markedly higher referral rate.**

---

## How to use

Paste the block below, then on the next line:

    ENGAGEMENT: <engagement name + reference>
    WEEK: <YYYY-WW or date range>
    ACTIVITY: <what you did this week — git log, work notes, completed deliverables>
    UPCOMING: <what's planned next week>
    BLOCKERS: <anything waiting on client or external dependency>
    RECIPIENT: <primary contact + their preference>

---

## The prompt (copy from here)

```
You are producing a weekly client status report. The §6 REPORT is
the deliverable, sent same day, same time, every week (typically
Friday afternoon or Monday morning). Three rules:

(1) PROGRESS, NOT ACTIVITY. "We had three meetings" is activity.
    "We completed the security findings inventory; 14 findings
    identified, 5 critical" is progress. Lead with progress.
(2) BLOCKERS SURFACED EARLY. Anything waiting on client is
    surfaced in §3, not buried. Clients can't unblock what they
    don't see.
(3) SHORT. Status reports >1 page get skimmed. Aim for ≤300 words
    body + bullets.

1. PROGRESS  (lead with this)
   What measurably moved this week. Per item:
     - Activity (one line, past tense, observable verb)
     - Output / artefact produced
     - Status (Complete / In Progress %)
   3–7 items. More signals noise; fewer signals stalled engagement.

2. UPCOMING NEXT WEEK
   What you'll do next week. 3–5 items, ordered by priority.
   Same format as §1.

3. BLOCKERS / NEEDS  ← high-leverage section
   Anything waiting on client or external:
     - What you need
     - From whom
     - By when (date, not "soon")
     - What happens if late
   If nothing is blocking, write "No blockers — all on track."
   honestly. Inventing blockers to look busy is a trust killer.

4. METRICS  (where measurable)
   - Hours used this week vs budgeted
   - % of total engagement complete
   - Days vs milestone
   Be honest about over-budget weeks — easier to discuss in
   weekly increments than in a single mid-engagement reckoning.

5. DECISIONS NEEDED  (if any)
   Decisions blocking progress that the client side must make:
     - Decision required
     - Options + recommendation
     - Decision deadline (when does silence become a blocker)

6. REPORT  ← the deliverable
   --- REPORT START ---
   **Subject:** <Engagement> Status — Week of <date>

   Hi <first name>,

   Quick weekly update on <ENGAGEMENT>.

   **This week**
   - <§1 progress items as bullets>

   **Next week**
   - <§2 plan items as bullets>

   **What we need from you**
   - <§3 blockers / asks; or "Nothing this week — all on track.">

   **Decisions to make**
   - <§5 if applicable; or omit>

   **At a glance**
   - Engagement progress: <X%> complete
   - On track for <next milestone date>

   Reply with any questions, or we'll catch up at our standing
   call on <day>.

   <your name>
   --- REPORT END ---

HARD RULES
- Progress (§1) comes from real activity log — never inflate to
  fill the section. A genuine slow week is honestly reported as
  such, paired with the §3 blocker that caused it.
- §3 blockers are time-bound. "Soon" is not a date.
- ≤300 words body. Long status reports go unread.
- Same day, same time, every week. Cadence reliability matters
  more than report depth.
- Respond in the language of the RECIPIENT input.

ENGAGEMENT:
WEEK:
ACTIVITY:
UPCOMING:
BLOCKERS:
RECIPIENT:
```

## What you get

A 30-minute Friday afternoon habit that transforms client perception
of the engagement. Clients who receive weekly status reports refer
3–5× more often than clients who get a final report only — because
they FEEL the work happening week by week.
