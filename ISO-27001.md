# ISO-27001 — ISMS Control Mapping for Certification (verkaufbares Deliverable)

Maps a system to ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A controls (93 controls in 4
themes) and drafts a Statement of Applicability (SoA). Pre-certification
gap analysis or surveillance-audit preparation.

**Realistic engagement price: €5 000 – 25 000.** Often recurring annually for surveillance.

---

## How to use

Paste the block below, then on the next line:

    SCOPE: <ISMS scope: which system, which information assets, which locations>
    STAGE: <PRE_CERT | STAGE_1_PREP | STAGE_2_PREP | SURVEILLANCE | RECERT>
    CONTEXT: <industry, regulatory backdrop, why ISO 27001 (customer ask / RFP gate / strategic)>
    CLIENT: <legal entity name>

## The prompt (copy from here)

```
You are producing an ISO/IEC 27001:2022 control assessment. The §8
REPORT is the deliverable, plus a draft Statement of Applicability
(SoA) in §7. Three rules:

(1) ISO 27001 is about the ISMS, not just the controls. The clauses
    4–10 management-system requirements matter as much as Annex A.
    Reviewers reject SoAs that lack ISMS context.
(2) Each Annex A control gets: applicable yes/no, implementation
    status, justification for inclusion/exclusion, link to evidence.
(3) "Implemented" requires evidence — code, config, policy, or
    documented procedure. Auditor question: "show me."

1. SCOPE & CONTEXT
   ISMS scope statement (per Clause 4.3): boundary of applicability,
   interfaces, dependencies, information assets in scope, locations,
   business units. Restate the business context driving certification
   (customer requirement, RFP gate, strategic positioning, regulatory).

2. MANAGEMENT SYSTEM CLAUSES  (4–10)
   For each, evidence visible (in code/config/repo docs) or gap:
     - Clause 4: Context, scope, ISMS
     - Clause 5: Leadership, policy, roles & responsibilities
     - Clause 6: Risk management process, risk treatment, objectives
     - Clause 7: Resources, competence, awareness, communication,
       documented information
     - Clause 8: Operational planning and control, risk assessment,
       risk treatment
     - Clause 9: Monitoring, measurement, analysis, internal audit,
       management review
     - Clause 10: Nonconformity, corrective action, continual
       improvement
   Most of these live outside code (policies, minutes); flag what is
   visible vs what requires the client's QMS / GRC tooling.

3. ANNEX A CONTROLS — ORGANISATIONAL  (37 controls, A.5.x)
   Walk each A.5 control:
     - A.5.1 Information security policies
     - A.5.2 Information security roles and responsibilities
     - ... (through A.5.37 Documented operating procedures)
   Per control: APPLICABLE (Y/N), STATUS (IMPLEMENTED / PARTIAL /
   PLANNED / NOT APPLICABLE), Evidence (file / config / policy ref),
   Justification (especially for N/A — reviewer reads these).

4. ANNEX A CONTROLS — PEOPLE  (8 controls, A.6.x)
   A.6.1 Screening through A.6.8 Reporting information security events.
   Most are organisational; note what code reveals (e.g. onboarding /
   offboarding automation).

5. ANNEX A CONTROLS — PHYSICAL  (14 controls, A.7.x)
   A.7.1 Physical security perimeters through A.7.14 Secure disposal.
   For cloud-hosted systems, most are inherited from CSP — document
   the inheritance model (CSP's ISO 27001 attestation acts as
   compensating evidence).

6. ANNEX A CONTROLS — TECHNOLOGICAL  (34 controls, A.8.x)  ← code-audit core
   Per control, evidence from code/config:
     - A.8.1 User endpoint devices
     - A.8.2 Privileged access rights (RBAC, secrets management)
     - A.8.3 Information access restriction
     - A.8.5 Secure authentication
     - A.8.6 Capacity management
     - A.8.7 Protection against malware
     - A.8.8 Management of technical vulnerabilities
     - A.8.9 Configuration management
     - A.8.10 Information deletion
     - A.8.11 Data masking
     - A.8.12 Data leakage prevention
     - A.8.13 Information backup
     - A.8.14 Redundancy of information processing facilities
     - A.8.15 Logging
     - A.8.16 Monitoring activities
     - A.8.17 Clock synchronisation
     - A.8.18 Use of privileged utility programs
     - A.8.19 Installation of software on operational systems
     - A.8.20 Networks security
     - A.8.21 Security of network services
     - A.8.22 Segregation of networks
     - A.8.23 Web filtering
     - A.8.24 Use of cryptography
     - A.8.25 Secure development life cycle
     - A.8.26 Application security requirements
     - A.8.27 Secure system architecture and engineering principles
     - A.8.28 Secure coding
     - A.8.29 Security testing in development and acceptance
     - A.8.30 Outsourced development
     - A.8.31 Separation of development, test and production environments
     - A.8.32 Change management
     - A.8.33 Test information
     - A.8.34 Protection of information systems during audit testing

7. STATEMENT OF APPLICABILITY  (draft)
   Tabular SoA, one row per Annex A control:
     | Control | Title | Applicable | Status | Justification | Evidence |
   This becomes the client's official SoA after their CISO / DPO
   adopts it. Mark every NOT APPLICABLE with reasoning; auditors flag
   missing justifications.

8. REPORT  ← the deliverable
   --- REPORT START ---
   # ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Control Assessment — <ISMS Scope>
   **Client:** <legal entity>   **Stage:** <pre-cert / stage 1 / etc.>
   **Assessment date:** <…>   **System SHA:** <…>

   ## Executive Summary             (≤300 words: certification readiness,
                                     top 5 gaps, estimated months to
                                     audit-ready)
   ## ISMS Scope & Context          (§1)
   ## Management System Readiness   (§2, clauses 4-10)
   ## Annex A — Organisational      (§3, table)
   ## Annex A — People              (§4)
   ## Annex A — Physical            (§5, with CSP inheritance map)
   ## Annex A — Technological       (§6 — main body)
   ## Statement of Applicability    (§7, full table)
   ## Gap Roadmap                   (gaps ordered by stage-2 audit
                                     blocker risk)
   ## Recommended Internal Audit    (sample test plan for §9 clauses)
   ## Limitations                   (§9, verbatim)
   --- REPORT END ---

9. LIMITATIONS & DISCLAIMER  (mandatory, verbatim with substitutions)
   "This assessment was produced by an AI agent from static analysis
    of the system at commit <SHA> on <date>. It is NOT a certification
    audit. ISO/IEC 27001 certification can only be issued by an
    accredited certification body following a two-stage audit. This
    report assesses readiness against the standard and drafts a
    Statement of Applicability for the client's CISO/DPO to adopt.
    Management-system requirements (Clauses 4–10) require organisational
    review including policies, minutes, training records, and management
    review evidence beyond code-visible artefacts. Items marked
    IMPLEMENTED reflect code or configuration evidence; operational
    effectiveness requires interview and sample-test evidence collected
    by the certification body. Surveillance audits in years 2 and 3
    re-test against this standard — maintain the SoA accordingly."

HARD RULES
- Every IMPLEMENTED claim has an Evidence cell. Empty Evidence = PARTIAL
  or PLANNED, never IMPLEMENTED.
- Every NOT APPLICABLE control has a Justification. ISO auditors read
  these specifically.
- Cloud-inherited controls (most of A.7) explicitly cite the CSP's ISO
  27001 attestation as evidence of inheritance.
- The SoA in §7 is the deliverable the client will publish — quality
  here is what auditors see first.
- Respond in the language of the SCOPE input.

SCOPE:
STAGE:
CONTEXT:
CLIENT:
```

## What the buyer gets

A pre-cert gap analysis with a draft SoA that maps cleanly to what the
certification body's Stage 1 audit will review. Most consultancies
charge €15–30k for this; a precise AI-assisted draft sells at €5–15k
with faster turnaround.
